# Summary figure caption

**Technical stability of Golgi-labelled image structures across five acquisition rounds.** Each point and pale line represents an acquisition field (five fields, potentially containing multiple nuclei). The indigo curve is the mean across fields; the band is a percentile-bootstrap 95% confidence interval from 10,000 resamples of fields within each round. These intervals do not include segmentation uncertainty or establish independent biological replication. Round 2 is the registration and overlap reference. Round 1 is an exposure-sensitivity condition, retained explicitly; native integrated signal is divided by target exposure time. Panels report gradient similarity, Jaccard overlap, expanded-space volume, compactness, median distance to accepted nuclear surfaces, and native background-corrected integrated signal per millisecond. Absolute values are shown; no within-field round-2 normalization is applied.

Registered morphology uses one 3D Otsu mask after plane-median background subtraction, normalized Gaussian smoothing (sigma 0.1083 expanded µm), and removal of 26-connected components smaller than 0.1 expanded µm³ (29 voxels). The complete DAPI foreground is excluded voxelwise. Nuclear foreground uses plane-background normalization and the Lamin watershed approach. Original source code and parameter definitions are in `docs/golgi-otsu-methods.md`; full threshold and size sensitivity is preserved in `results/golgi-otsu-component-study-20260923/`.

Small rejected puncta may include genuine Golgi fragments. Projection overlap with a nuclear outline does not imply 3D nuclear inclusion; same-Z overlays and exact mask audits are the relevant checks. Completion and mask audits alone do not prove biological specificity.

Native signal uses an independently segmented DAPI-defined exterior ROI within 18 expanded µm of the complete native nuclear foreground. It applies the same smoothing, threshold and component rules, but integrates the original unsmoothed pixels after subtraction of recorded plane backgrounds. The native ROI differs from the old full-field signal domain; differences from the old analysis are methodological, not biological.
