**Fig. X | Quantification of lamin fluorescence across five acquisition rounds.** Analysis completed for all five datasets (Cell1–Cell5; upper left). Quantitative panels include 11 quality-controlled nuclei from three datasets in round 1 and 15 nuclei from five datasets in rounds 2–5; existing round-1 QC exclusions for Cell1 and Cell4 were retained. Surface coverage (upper middle) is the fraction of sampled nuclear-surface vertices with a supported lamin fluorescence ridge. Continuity (upper right) is the fraction of all sampled vertices in the largest connected region of supported signal. Ridge full-width at half-maximum (FWHM; lower left) is the median width of supported fluorescence profiles sampled along nuclear-surface normals, in micrometers. Integrated ridge intensity (lower middle) is the median background-corrected profile integral per nucleus, divided by target exposure time and normalized to the corresponding nucleus in acquisition round 2: (I_r/t_r)/(I_2/t_2). Target exposure was 150 ms in round 1 and 200 ms in rounds 2–5, with target laser power fixed at 40%. This correction assumes linear detector response and does not remove exposure-dependent differences in signal detectability. The dashed line denotes unity. Colored points represent nuclei, gray lines connect the same nucleus across rounds, and blue lines and shaded bands indicate arithmetic means and pointwise 95% percentile-bootstrap confidence intervals (10,000 resamples of nuclei per round). Round-specific means use the available QC-included nuclei, so the round-1 comparison also reflects a change in cohort composition. Nuclei are nested within datasets; intervals represent nucleus-level sampling uncertainty without accounting for within-dataset dependence. FWHM describes apparent fluorescent-ridge width rather than physical lamina thickness.
